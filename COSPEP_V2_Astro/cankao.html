Executive Summary Report

Unlocking the Potential of Stapled Peptides

Natural peptides hold immense therapeutic promise but are limited by rapid degradation in the body and an inability to enter cells. Stapled peptide technology solves this by synthetically "locking" peptides into their active shapes, revolutionizing drug discovery for previously "undruggable" targets.

Proteolytic Resistance

>12x Increase

Natural peptides are rapidly degraded by enzymes in the bloodstream, resulting in half-lives of minutes. The hydrocarbon staple acts as a physical shield, preventing proteases from accessing and cleaving the peptide backbone, extending half-life to hours or days.

Key Takeaway:

Allows for practical dosing schedules in clinical settings rather than continuous infusion.

Therapeutic Horizons

Because stapled peptides can now enter cells and remain stable, they unlock a massive class of "undruggable" intracellular targets, primarily protein-protein interactions (PPIs) that traditional small molecules cannot block.

⚕

Oncology

Reactivating tumor suppressor pathways. The most prominent example is stapled peptides designed to block the p53-MDM2 interaction, forcing cancer cells into apoptosis (programmed cell death).

🦠

Infectious Disease

Targeting viral fusion mechanisms. Stapled peptides can bind to viral envelope proteins (like in HIV or RSV), preventing the virus from merging with and entering healthy human cells.

⚙

Metabolic Disorders

Creating long-acting incretin mimetics. Stapling GLP-1 and dual agonists increases their stability, potentially leading to highly effective, less frequently dosed treatments for obesity and diabetes.
